See the bigger picture with the Open Science and Data Platform

Helping Canadians learn about major projects through maps, science and open data. 

Home / Initiatives / See the bigger picture with the Open Science and Data Platform

Highlights
  • The Open Science and Data Platform (OSDP) brings together thousands of government datasets, scientific publications, socio-economic information, environmental indicators, and regulatory records in one online platform.
  • It improves transparency, supports informed decision-making, and helps streamline development project planning and impact assessment.
  • Information about major projects, cumulative effects, and impact assessments is no longer spread across many organizations and databases. With the OSDP, government science and data are easier to find and use – all through interactive maps, creating value for Canadians.

Context

The Open Science and Data Platform (OSDP) helps Canadians find and use information to inform their understanding about impact assessments and major development projects. At the core of the platform are interactive maps and search tools that allow users to explore information by location.

The OSDP is led by Natural Resources Canada (NRCan), with support from Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC) and in partnership with federal, provincial, and territorial governments. It makes information that was once distributed across multiple websites and databases easier to access.

Canadians can use the OSDP to explore major projects, such as proposed mines, transportation corridors, energy developments, and other projects undergoing impact assessment. Interactive maps help users discover and visualize environmental conditions, land use, and cumulative effects within a specific region.

The OSDP serves as a single-window platform for geospatial data, scientific publications, environmental monitoring information, regulatory records, and regional content collections. Launched in 2021 in response to public calls for greater transparency and easier access to information, the platform supports impact assessment and informed decision-making for major projects.

Today, the OSDP serves a wide range of users, including project proponents, regulators, Indigenous communities, municipalities, researchers, policymakers, environmental organizations, and members of the public. Whether they are participating in an impact assessment or simply learning about development in their region, users can access authoritative information relevant to their needs.

The challenge

Major projects deliver important economic and community benefits. At the same time, they can have impacts that must be understood and managed in advance. People need access to reliable information to understand these projects and participate in decisions about them.

In the past, information needed to support decision-making was spread across many government websites, databases, and organizations. Finding and gathering trusted information could be difficult and time-consuming. Users struggled to locate reliable information for a specific project or region.

Golden Triangle in northwestern British Columbia

The OSDP’s Golden Triangle collection brings together geospatial data, scientific information, environmental indicators and regulatory records into a single map-based interface for one of Canada’s most active resource development regions.

Inability to find and visualize relevant information raised concerns for regulators, project proponents, Indigenous communities, researchers, and members of the public alike. Limited access to authoritative science and data could slow project planning and assessment, result in duplication, and make it harder for Canadians to understand how decisions were being made.

As Canada advances major infrastructure, energy transition, and critical minerals projects, access to trusted science and geospatial information has become increasingly important.

The geospatial solution

The OSDP addresses these challenges by organizing trusted science, geospatial information, environmental and socio-economic data, and regulatory information together in a single online platform. Rather than duplicating existing systems, it connects trusted information from multiple government organizations and public registries, enabling users to find what they need in one place.

Users can explore locations across Canada with the OSDP’s interactive maps and quickly access science and data products related to that area. Interactive maps and regional content collections help users see how different sources of information connect within a specific place.

Resources to support assessment of major projects in northwestern British Columbia

Northwestern British Columbia is one of Canada’s most active mineral development regions. Proposed major projects often raise different questions for different audiences.

  • Indigenous communities may seek to understand potential effects on their lands and waters
  • Local governments may consider infrastructure needs and regional growth
  • Regulators and project proponents require access to authoritative scientific and regulatory information
  • Residents may want to understand how a project fits within the broader landscape

The OSDP’s “Resources to Support Assessment of Major Projects in Northwestern British Columbia” content collection brings together authoritative maps, scientific studies, environmental information, and regulatory records in one place.

It supports cumulative effects assessment, early project planning, Indigenous engagement, and informed public participation by making trusted information easier to discover and use.

Explore the collection on the OSDP

The platform also supports more informed participation in impact assessment and planning processes. With its location-based science and comprehensive data, the OSDP helps Indigenous communities, local governments, regulators, project proponents, researchers, and the public use the same trusted information. This creates a more transparent and consistent foundation for discussing development opportunities, cumulative effects, and potential environmental, social, and economic impacts.

The Canada Centre for Mapping and Earth Observation (CCMEO) developed geospatial infrastructure and standards that helped shape the OSDP. These standards ensure consistency in data discovery and sharing across organizations and jurisdictions, making it easier to connect diverse data sources into a seamless user experience.

Benefits for Canadians

  • Better access to science and data, and regulatory information
  • Greater transparency
  • More efficient impact assessments
  • Better understanding of cumulative effects
  • Less duplication

These benefits help Canadians better understand development projects and participate more effectively in decisions that affect their communities.

Canadians can use this tool to understand what development is considered in their region and what the impacts could be

— Colter Kelly, Senior Impact Assessment Office, Natural Resources Canada

The OSDP advances Open Government principles by providing public access to science and data, with user satisfaction exceeding 80 percent.

Big fan of this open science effort, good job Canada!

— Founder and CEO, Canadian technology firm

By making authoritative science and data easier to discover, explore and understand, the OSDP supports informed public participation, improves transparency, and helps Canadians better understand the environmental, social, and economic context of development decisions.

Together, these outcomes demonstrate how modern geospatial infrastructure creates public value, not simply by providing more data, but by making trusted information easier to discover, understand, and use.

Why this matters to Canadians

The OSDP demonstrates how geospatial information creates practical value for Canadians by:

  • helping people better understand development in their communities
  • making trusted government science and data easier to find and use
  • improving transparency around major projects and impact assessments
  • supporting more informed conversations and decisions
  • reducing duplication by connecting information from many organizations through one interactive platform
